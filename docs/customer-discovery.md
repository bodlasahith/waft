# Waft — Customer Discovery Script

> **The job of these conversations is to find out whether the "what's your Instagram?" pain is
> real and hair-on-fire, and whether the host or the individual feels it more.** Not to pitch.
> The YC staffer's advice: validate the pain before building more. This script exists so you
> walk into Pear (and the Uber rides, and lunch) ready to *learn*, not sell.

## The 5 rules (from *The Mom Test*) — reread before every conversation

1. **Talk about their life, not your idea.** The moment they know what you're building, they start
   being polite and their data becomes worthless.
2. **Ask about the past, not the future.** "What did you do at the last event?" beats "Would you
   use an app that…?" People are terrible predictors of their own future behavior and great
   reporters of what they actually did.
3. **Ask for specifics, not generics.** "Tell me about the last person you exchanged contacts with"
   > "How do you usually network?"
4. **Dig for real cost.** Pain is real when they've already spent time, money, or workarounds on it.
   "What have you tried?" / "What did that cost you?" reveals whether it's hair-on-fire or mild.
5. **Shut up and listen.** Talk <30% of the time. Silence after a question makes them fill it with
   truth. Don't lead the witness.

**Bad questions (never ask these):** "Would you use this?" · "Do you think this is a good idea?" ·
"How much would you pay for X?" · "Would you pay for an app that does Y?" — all invite polite lies.

## Track A — Event hosts / organizers (the wedge)

*Goal: is helping attendees connect a real problem the host owns — enough to set something up?*

**Warm-up (their world):**
- Tell me about the last event you ran — who came, what was the point?
- What does a *great* one look like vs. a flop? What makes the difference?

**The pain (past behavior):**
- What do you do *today* to help attendees actually meet each other? Walk me through it.
- Last event — did people connect the way you hoped? What got in the way?
- Have you tried anything to fix that? Name tags, apps, icebreakers, seating, a Slack/WhatsApp?
  What happened — did people use it?
- What do attendees complain about after? What do *you* wish went better?

**Cost / intensity:**
- How much of your prep time goes to "will people click"? Is it a top-3 worry or a nice-to-have?
- Have you ever paid for anything to help with networking (tools, staff, a facilitator)?

**Listen for the lean-forward:** an unprompted "god, yes, people never actually meet" · a workaround
they *built themselves* · asking *you* "wait, how would that work?" before you've pitched.

## Track B — Individual heavy networkers (the moat / retention)

*Goal: is the contact-exchange moment actually painful, or am I projecting?*

**The core behavior (be specific):**
- Think about the last person you met at an event you wanted to stay in touch with. What did you
  actually do in that moment? Walk me through it step by step.
- Which app did you end up trading? Why that one? Did you ever second-guess it?
- How many contacts from the last event do you think you actually still talk to? Why the drop-off?

**Workarounds / cost:**
- Do you have a system — a link in bio, a Linktree, a saved photo of a QR, a note you paste?
  Why'd you set that up? (a self-built workaround = real pain)
- Ever lost a contact you wanted because the exchange was awkward or you picked the wrong platform?
- When someone asks "what's your Instagram?" and you barely use it — what happens?

**Frequency (this decides retention):**
- How often does this even come up — weekly, a few times a year? (rare = weak individual pull)

**Listen for the lean-forward:** they've already built a workaround · visible frustration recounting
a specific botched exchange · "oh my god yes" energy, unprompted.

## Track C — The integration probe (do things that don't scale)

*Don't ask if they'd want an integration — offer the real thing and watch what they do.*

- To a host, after Track A: *"I could set up a live networking graph for your next event as a free
  add-on — QR on the tables, a screen showing the room connect in real time. Want to try it?"*
- **The signal is the yes + follow-through**, not the polite "sounds cool." A real yes = a date and
  a room. That single commitment is worth more than 20 flattering interviews.

## Capturing signal (do this within an hour, while it's fresh)

Per conversation, jot:
- **Who** (host / individual, event type, size)
- **The most surprising thing they said** (verbatim if possible)
- **Did they lean forward? On what exactly?** (the trigger, not just yes/no)
- **A real cost they've already paid** (time/money/workaround) — or none
- **Would they take a next step?** (try the wall / intro to another host / just chat) — commitment > compliment

## The decision this feeds

After ~15–20: **which group leaned forward more, and on what pain?** That answers host-vs-individual —
the open question from the positioning reframe. Don't average the politeness; count the lean-forwards.
One host who books a date > ten "cool idea"s.

_Method: The Mom Test (Rob Fitzpatrick). Tailored to Waft's host-vs-individual sequencing question._
